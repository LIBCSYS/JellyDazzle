# 143 — Caustic Pool

**Look.** The light net on the floor of a swimming pool: bright rippling cell walls with
darker cell interiors and near-black water between, the whole net drifting and
reconnecting. Warm on one edge, cool on the other, like dispersion in real water.

**Math.** Four sinusoidal surface waves (wavelengths 32–66 grid px, steepness `S = 1.25`,
directions themselves slowly wobbling) define `h(x,y,t)`. Each cell of a 320x240 grid
launches four photons (2x2 supersampled, quarter weight, plus a static sub-pixel jitter
tile to break the launch lattice), refracted to `(x - G dh/dx, y - G dh/dy)`. The
important part is the photon *weight*: the Jacobian of the refraction map is available
analytically — `Jxx = 1 - S sum a_i u_i^2 sin(p_i)` and so on — and each photon carries
`1/(0.045 + |det J|) - 3.2`, clamped at zero. Only source cells sitting on a fold of the
map (`det J = 0`, exactly where real caustics form) emit anything, so the floor stays
black instead of washing into a flat field. Two sets at 10% different refraction
strengths stand in for chromatic dispersion. Separable 1-2-1 blur, tone map, bilinear
upscale over a dark depth-shaded water ground.
