# 166 — Star Trails

A long exposure pointed near the celestial pole, and the only pattern here with no
drawn geometry at all — the arcs are an artefact of the exposure, not of any curve. 900
stars are distributed uniformly in area (`r = sqrt(u)`) about an off-centre pole with a
cubed magnitude distribution (a few bright, many faint) and rotate rigidly at one
revolution per 9000 frames. Each frame every star deposits light at its current
position and the whole plate is multiplied by 0.9976, which is the entire mechanism:
the exponential fade sets the trail length (about 400 frames of visible tail, roughly
16 degrees of arc), and because a star's *dwell* per pixel is inversely proportional to
its radius, stars near the pole crawl, accumulate more, and read as short bright arcs
while stars at the frame edge sweep and thin — the correct photographic behaviour, for
free. The nine brightest stars get the 5-tap stroke instead of a bare splat, which
gives them a small halo and reads as diffraction. On the very first call the plate is
primed with 600 frames of history sampled every third frame at compensated weight
`0.125 * 3 * decay^age`, so the trails are already there when the layer fades in
instead of growing over ten seconds; after that the plate is never cleared, since the
rotation is continuous and the old plate is exactly what a real exposure would hold.
The pole drifts a few pixels over tens of thousands of frames, which very slightly
feathers the oldest arcs. Measured: ~305 fps at 1280x960, mean frame delta **0.04** —
the calmest pattern measured in the library — and 84% near-black. Ideal SCREEN overlay.
