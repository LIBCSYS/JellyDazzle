# 170 — Gyroid Veil

A moving slice through a triply-periodic minimal surface. The Schoen gyroid is the zero
set of `F = sin x cos y + sin y cos z + sin z cos x`; hold `z` and you get a labyrinth
of curves in the plane, and letting `z` drift at 0.0016 rad/frame turns that labyrinth
into something that continuously splits, reconnects and re-knits — the reason it never
"loops" or snaps is that you are watching a genuine three-dimensional surface pass
through the frame, not a 2D field being interpolated. With `z` fixed the expression
collapses to `sin x cos y + C1 sin y + C2 cos x`, which is separable in the two screen
coordinates, and since a rotated/scaled sampling grid varies *linearly* along a row,
the whole inner loop is two fixed-point phase accumulators, four reads from a
1024-entry sine table and three multiplies — no transcendentals, no division, no
square root per pixel. Two contours are drawn rather than one: a bright filament at
`|F| = 0` and a second at `||F| - 0.62| = 0` (the level breathes), each through a
Lorentzian core table `255/(1 + 78 a^2)`, so the picture is line work with a doubled
wall and a black interior instead of a filled field. Hue is driven directly by the
signed field value, which tints the two sides of every wall differently and is what
gives the surface its apparent relief; a radial vignette closes the frame, and the
sampling plane rotates 0.00021 rad/frame and breathes +/-14% in scale. Measured: ~250
fps at 1280x960, mean frame delta 0.23, 66% near-black.
