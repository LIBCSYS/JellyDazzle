# 192 — Steiner Pearls

Steiner chains, rolling under a circle inversion. A Steiner chain is the exact answer to
"n equal circles, each tangent to its two neighbours and to two fixed circles", and it
only closes when the annulus ratio obeys `sin(pi/n) = (R-r)/(R+r)`. Fix `R+r = 2` and the
whole necklace falls out of n alone: n pearls of radius `s = sin(pi/n)`, centres on the
unit circle, running between guide circles of radius `1 ± s`. Three such chains are built
at 1.00, 0.72 and 0.44 scale with independently diced n from 6 to 17, counter-rotating at
0.0009 to 0.0020 radians a frame. Every circle is then pushed through an inversion of
radius-squared 3.1 to 5.7 about a centre orbiting at distance 2.4 to 3.6 — deliberately
outside the outermost guide circle, so no denominator can cross zero and the interior maps
to the interior, which means the picture cannot blow up and needs no clamping. Inversion
carries circles to circles exactly, so the chains stay tangent while their radii slide
around the ring: pearls swell on one side and pack tight on the other, a slow tide with a
period of about three and a half minutes. Each pearl gets a rim, an inner highlight and a
centre spark; the guide circles stay at a tenth the weight. The frame is fitted to the
image of the outer guide circle, so the composition never drifts off screen. 228 fps,
mean frame delta 0.27, 93% near-black — overlay, and one of the sparsest in the set.
