# 177 — Sandpile Mandala

The abelian sandpile relaxing on camera. A disc of 64-86 cells is loaded with 7-12 grains
each — far more than the four a cell can hold — and then left to topple: any cell with
four or more grains gives one to each of its four neighbours. The rule is *abelian*, which
is the property the whole routine leans on: the final configuration does not depend on the
order of topplings, so the pile can be relaxed six Gauss-Seidel sweeps per frame over a
tracked active bounding box and still converge to the one true answer, spread over a
34-second segment instead of a single hitching frame. What emerges is not a blur but the
famous fractal medallion — flat plateaus of constant height, straight facet boundaries,
triangular and square patches repeating self-similarly at every scale, all of it inside an
octagonal outline that grows outward frame by frame from the middle of a black field. Two
things make it watchable. Cells that are still in flight flicker between counts from sweep
to sweep, so the display is not the height array but a float buffer easing toward it at
0.10/frame — the eye sees a slow settle instead of a boil. And only four colours are ever
used, one per grain count, on a `0.18/0.44/0.70/0.94` brightness ladder: this is the stark
end of the palette taxonomy, flat poster colour with no gradient anywhere. Grid is
501x501, resampled bilinearly into a centred square. Resets at `sl == 0`. 77% near-black,
mean frame delta 0.1.
