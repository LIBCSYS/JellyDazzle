# 184 — Caustic Billiard

A long exposure of three balls bouncing forever inside one table. The wall is the
implicit superellipse (|x|/a)^p + (|y|/b)^p = 1; each frame every ball is
ray-marched to the wall and bisected onto it, the chord is stamped, and the
velocity is reflected in the true gradient normal, d′ = d − 2(d·n)n. Nothing is
faked, and the physics is the picture: at p = 2 the table is an ellipse, the
billiard is integrable, every orbit is tangent to one confocal caustic, and the
exposure therefore grows a bright caustic rim around an elliptical hole that is
never entered. Push p away from 2 and integrability breaks — the caustics smear
into whorls and the tangle slowly fills the table. Chords are laid down at very
low weight into a persistent canvas that decays 0.5% per frame, so brightness
accumulates exactly where orbits crowd, the way light does. The drawing frame
precesses about half a degree per second, so fresh strokes lie rotated against
the fading ones and the exposure turns into a rosette; everything is mirrored
four ways, the table's own symmetry group. Accumulator: clears at sl == 0 and
builds for the whole segment. 203 fps, delta 0.22.
