# 115 — Vega Bulge

Vasarely op-art: a flat checkerboard lattice of rounded cells that swells into a sphere
pressing out of the picture, with a second, smaller pinch drawing the lattice inward
elsewhere. Both are the same one-line trick — the pixel is mapped back through a radial
magnification field `s(r) = 1 + A/(1 + (r/sigma)^2)` before the lattice is looked up, so cells
grow toward the centre of a bulge and crowd at its rim. A is +1.35 for the bulge and -0.62
for the pinch, both breathing, both drifting on slow Lissajous paths. Each cell is drawn from
a superellipse distance `d = (fx^4 + fy^4)^(1/4)` against a radius that follows the local
magnification (0.30 + 0.14(s-1)), which is what sells the volume: swollen cells are not just
bigger, they are rounder and more brightly lit. Colour is a checkerboard of two palette hues
over a dark ground of a third, with a shading term proportional to s — deliberately stark,
two-tone, high contrast. The lattice also rotates about a degree every twenty-five seconds.
Full-bleed: a ground layer. 349 fps, mean delta 0.67.
