# 178 — Doyle Spiral

A hexagonal circle packing bent into a logarithmic spiral. Take the triangular lattice of
equal tangent circles in the w-plane and push it through the conformal map `z = exp(w)`.
Conformal means angles — and therefore tangencies — survive, so the circles stay circles
and stay in contact, but each is scaled by `|z|`: the packing becomes a spiral of discs
growing geometrically outward from the centre of the frame, the Doyle spiral. The lattice
spacing is chosen as `s = 2*pi/(q*sqrt3)` for q = 5-9 arms, which makes q lattice steps
close exactly one turn; the consequence is that the whole figure is invariant under a zoom
of `exp(s*q)`, so drifting w along the real axis is a **seamless endless zoom** — discs
bloom out of the singularity at the centre and leave at the corners, forever, with no
repeat seam. Angles come out as `n*pi/q` and moduli as `exp(s*(m + n/2))`, the half-step
offset being exactly what turns concentric rings into spiral arms. Discs are drawn as rims
with a one-pixel halo and energy normalised per unit arc; they are smoothstepped up out of
the singularity below 6 px and down again past 330 px so nothing pops in or out, and rings
entirely off-canvas are rejected before they are walked. 87% near-black, mean frame
delta 0.6.
