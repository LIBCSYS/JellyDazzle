# 163 — Droste Spiral

An Escher-style conformal zoom that is exactly seamless and never ends. Each pixel is
converted once, at init, to log-polar coordinates `(b, a) = (ln r, theta/2pi)`; the
picture is then a lattice laid out in *that* plane, not in the screen plane. The
lattice coordinates are `u = 9a + b/ln2` and `v = b/ln2`, both of which are built only
from integer multiples of the two natural periods — going once around the centre adds
9 to `u` and 0 to `v`, moving out one scale doubling adds 1 to each — so the tiling
closes on itself in both directions and there is no seam at `theta = 0` and no visible
step at any scale. Nine arms is not arbitrary: cells are square in the log-polar plane
when the arm count is near `2pi/ln 2 = 9.06`, which is what makes the beads round
rather than smeared. Each cell paints a bead `255/(1 + 120 d^6)` plus a thin connecting
web `255/(1 + 26 d^4)`, `d` being the distance to the cell centre, so the field reads
as a spiral of medallions strung on a filament rather than as a gradient. Time adds a
constant to `u` and `v` (a steady 1/1024-cell-per-frame drift, which because of the
log-polar map *is* an endless zoom, one doubling every ~975 frames) and a constant to
`theta` (a whole-field rotation, one turn every ~6000 frames); the two together give
the classic Droste screw. Hue walks with the lattice coordinates and a soft radial
vignette closes the frame. Everything after the one-off `logf`/`atan2f` is integer
work — shifts, masks and two table reads per pixel. The arm count is deliberately a
constant rather than seed-derived: it is the one parameter that cannot be crossfaded,
and a segment re-seed would have jumped the geometry. Measured: ~240 fps at 1280x960,
mean frame delta 0.38, 56-62% near-black, sigma 53 — the brightest and most
contrasty of this set, and the one intended as a ground layer.
