# 185 — Sandpile

The abelian sandpile, grown live. Fifteen grains a frame are dropped on the centre
cell of a 181×181 lattice; any cell holding four or more topples, sending one
grain to each of its four neighbours, and the avalanche is relaxed with fourteen
multi-topple (h>>2) sweeps per frame inside a tracked active bounding box. The
result is the famous theorem-with-a-picture: a completely deterministic,
perfectly D4-symmetric fractal mandala of triangular patches that looks the same
at every scale, with a radius that grows as √n and reaches the lattice edge right
about at the end of a segment. Heights 0..3 are the four colours and height 0 is
left black, so the pile reads as coloured lace on a void rather than as a filled
disc. The displayed field chases the integer heights with a ten-frame lag, which
turns every level change into a fade instead of a flip and drops the measured
motion to almost nothing; the lattice is then sampled through a slowly turning,
slowly breathing transform so the finished mandala keeps drifting after the
growth front has passed. Accumulator: clears at sl == 0. 232 fps (238 fps averaged
over a full 2048-frame segment), delta 0.13.
