# 146 — Apollonian Rings

**Look.** A circle packing drawn as jewellery: a large outer rim, three big circles
inside it, and the tangency gaps filled recursively by ever-smaller rings down to a few
pixels, each stroked as a neon ring with a soft halo and nothing filled. Pure black
between the rings. The whole gasket turns about a third of a degree per second and
breathes 4%, and every ring shimmers on its own slow phase.

**Math.** Generated once via the complex Descartes circle theorem in its linear form: a
circle is the vector `s = (b, b x, b y)` with bend `b = 1/r`, and the second circle
tangent to a triple is `s' = 2(s1+s2+s3) - s4` — a reflection, so the entire packing falls
out of one recursion with no square roots and no quadratic solve. The seed quadruple is
bends (-1, 2, 2, 3); recursion replaces each of the three older members in turn, stopping
at bend 172 or 640 circles. Each ring is scanned as an annulus (per row, two spans found
by two square roots), with coverage `(1 - d/2.1)^2` for the core plus `0.38 (1 - d/8)^2`
for the halo, added into the frame. Colour comes from `log(radius)` so ring size maps to
palette position; small circles carry the far end of the ramp.
