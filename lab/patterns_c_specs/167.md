# 167 — Medusa Drift

Bioluminescent jellyfish, which is what the app is named for and, oddly, was not yet in
the library. Four medusae drift on slow Lissajous paths at different rates. Each bell
is a parametric dome `x = cx + bw cos(a) s(a), y = cy - bh sin(a) s(a)` over
`a` in [0,pi], where `s(a) = 1 + 0.055 sin(8a + wt)` scallops the margin; the animal
swims by contracting, so a pulse phase `p = (1 + sin(w t + ph))/2` drives
`bw = R(0.84 + 0.30 p)` and `bh = R(1.30 - 0.42 p)` in antiphase — wide and flat on the
push, tall and narrow on the recovery — and adds a 9 px bob, which is enough for the
eye to read jet propulsion. The margin is closed with a shallow downward arc plus its
own ripple, five radial canals are drawn from margin to apex, and behind the bell hang
six tentacles and four shorter oral arms, each a travelling-wave filament
`x = x0 + (10 + 20v) v sin(5.3v - 0.021 t + phase)` whose amplitude grows with the
normalised arclength `v` and whose phase lags the bell, so the wave visibly runs down
the animal after each contraction. Brightness falls off toward every tip. Everything is
stamped with the 5-tap soft stroke into a plate decaying 0.86/frame, which is what
makes the filaments glow and smear slightly along their own motion instead of reading
as wire. The three colours per animal (bell, margin, tentacles) are 22 and 40 ramp
entries apart, so each medusa is one hue family and the four are spread across the
palette. Measured: ~275 fps at 1280x960, mean frame delta 0.52, 89% near-black — a
sparse overlay with a lot of empty water in it, which is exactly what a stacking layer
wants.
