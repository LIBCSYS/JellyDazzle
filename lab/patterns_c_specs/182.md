# 182 — Glass Weave

Leon Glass's random-dot moiré, animated. A fixed cloud of 3400 random points is
plotted together with three images of itself under a flow map: at each step the
point is displaced by a *fixed distance* along the direction A·p, where A is a
2×2 matrix whose four entries are independent slow sines. The eye cannot resolve
which dot pairs with which, but it locks instantly onto the correlation field, so
the dust self-organises into circulation, radial spray, spirals or hyperbolic
saddles with no line ever being drawn. Normalising the step length (rather than
using the raw linear map) keeps the streaks the same length at the rim as at the
centre, so the percept holds across the whole frame instead of dying at the
edges. Each dot carries a fixed hue drawn from a spatial wave, so the flow reads
as coloured filaments of dust; the later iterates are progressively dimmer, which
gives every dot a four-bead comet tail pointing along its own trajectory. The
cloud itself creeps at 0.0006 rad/frame, so nothing ever jumps, and the matrix
walks continuously through the entire Glass family and back. The sparsest routine
in the set — a few percent coverage on black. 219 fps, delta 0.89.
