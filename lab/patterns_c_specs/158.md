# 158 Blaze

**Look.** Bridget Riley's *Blaze*: concentric rings of radial bands whose angular phase
zig-zags from ring to ring, so a flat pattern reads as a deep twisted funnel. Rings
stream inward forever; the bands are one saturated accent hue against pure black with a
white-hot crest, and roughly half the frame sits at zero. The starkest routine in the
set — the one that answers "stark" in "stark to amazing colours".

**Math.** Everything happens in log-polar. Per pixel `u = k log r + v t` (with r, log r
and atan2 baked into tables at setup), so the ring lattice is periodic in u and streams
inward with no restart. Inside ring `floor(u)` the fractional part f shifts the band
angle by `+- amp (f - 0.5)/N`, sign alternating per ring — that alternation is Riley's
zig. The band field is `sin(N (theta + zig) + spin t)`, thresholded with a width-aware
antialias term `e = 1.9 N / r_px`: as the bands converge toward the centre the threshold
softens automatically, so the pattern greys out gracefully instead of aliasing, and a
radial fade blanks the last pixel or two. A ridge shade `1 - |2f-1|` across each ring
gives the bands their rolled, three-dimensional edge, and the band colour lerps between
two palette stops by the same sine, with a cubed white core. 9-19 sectors, zoom and spin
signs chosen per segment. 480x360, upscaled. Measured 244-283 fps, delta 1.38-1.79.
