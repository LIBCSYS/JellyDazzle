# 118 — Harmonic Lantern

A gauzy many-lobed solid turning in the dark, its surface built from thousands of points so
that it glows where the surface folds toward the eye and thins to gauze where it turns away;
over minutes it melts from a six-lobed clover into a spiked urchin into a pinched torus.
The radius is Bourke's spherical-harmonic surface,
`r = |sin(m0 phi)|^2 + |cos(m1 phi)|^3 + |sin(m2 theta)|^2 + |cos(m3 theta)|^3`, which
separates cleanly: the phi terms are a per-row table and the theta terms a per-column table,
so each of the 420x240 samples costs two adds and a handful of multiplies. The frequencies
must be integers — any other value tears the surface where theta wraps — so each of the two
pairs cross-fades to a new random integer on its own slow clock, both members of a pair
swapping on the same frame (swapping them independently was worth 10 units of frame delta and
the harness found it). Points are yawed, pitched with a slowly nodding camera,
perspective-projected and deposited additively with weight k^2 for the near-field falloff; hue
comes from radius and depth. 80-82% near-black — an overlay. 188 fps, mean delta 0.52.
