# 181 — Icosahedral Mirror

A kaleidoscope built on the sphere instead of the plane. Every screen pixel is
lifted onto the unit sphere by the inverse stereographic map
P = (2x, 2y, q−1)/(q+1) with q = x²+y², and then measured against the fifteen
mirror planes of the icosahedral group I_h — the three coordinate axes plus the
twelve (±1, ±1/φ, ±φ)/2 and their cyclic permutations, i.e. the normals of the
group's fifteen two-fold axes. The distance to the nearest plane draws a
Lorentzian filament, w²/(w²+d²); the distance to the *second* nearest lights the
crossings, so the 120-triangle disdyakis web appears as glowing wire with
jewelled 2-, 3- and 5-fold junctions rather than as a flat line drawing. Line
width is scaled by the stereographic Jacobian 2/(1+q), which keeps a filament the
same thickness on screen from the pole out to the rim and removes the aliasing
that a constant angular width would produce at the edge of frame. The sphere
turns under three detuned slow rotations (≈0.1°/frame), a travelling spherical
wave walks hue along the wire, and a front-facing term sinks the far hemisphere
into the black. 480×360 evaluation, bilinear upscale. Pure line art: ~90% of the
frame is near-zero, so it MAX-composites straight over any ground. 149 fps,
frame-to-frame delta 0.35.
