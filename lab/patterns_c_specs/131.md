# 131 Times Table

**Look.** A round string-art medallion: several hundred straight chords stretched across a
disc, so dense that the eye stops seeing the individual threads and sees only where they
bunch — a scalloped, many-lobed envelope with sharp cusps, wrapped in a second, smaller
envelope turning the other way. The lobes count up and down over minutes: eight petals
becomes nine becomes ten, the new cusp always being born at the rim and walking inward.
Hue sweeps around the rim so opposite sides of the medallion sit in different parts of the
palette, with a slow brightness wave running around the circle. It is the one dense,
frame-filling piece in this batch (dark 0.47) and works as a ground under sparser layers.

**Math.** The 19th-century multiplication-table construction: mark points at `theta_k =
2 pi k / N` on a circle and join point `k` to point `m k`. The chords envelope an
epicycloid with `m - 1` cusps — `m = 2` gives a cardioid, `m = 3` a nephroid — and the
whole pattern is that caustic, not the chords. Here `m` is a *real* number crawling on a
slow sine between about 2 and 13, so the epicycloid is never finished and no modulus is
ever taken: the endpoint is `cos(m theta)`, and the wrap is carried by the cosine itself,
which is why nothing snaps at integer `m`. Two families run at once (`m_A` and a slower
`m_B` on a 0.68x radius, counter-rotating) in two parts of the palette. Chords are
rasterised additively at a fixed 1.5 samples/pixel so long and short chords deposit equal
energy per unit length; the per-chord weight is deliberately low (0.115) and the count high
(900 + 540) so the image is dominated by the bunching rather than by any single thread —
which is also what keeps the frame-to-frame delta in budget while `m` drifts. 384x288 float
canvas, 3x3 bloom, exponential tone map, bilinear upscale. Measured 284 fps at 1280x960,
delta 2.56-3.06.
