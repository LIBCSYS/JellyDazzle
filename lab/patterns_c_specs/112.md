# 112 — Vortex Ink

Ink dropped into slowly stirred water: sheets of colour fold over themselves, stretch into
filaments, roll up into spirals and marble together at their edges. It is an actual fluid,
not a painted imitation — Stam's stable-fluids solver on a 208x156 grid. Velocity and dye are
both moved by semi-Lagrangian advection (trace back one step, bilinear sample), then the
velocity is projected onto its divergence-free part: build `div u`, solve `lap p = div u`
with 16 Jacobi iterations, subtract `grad p`. A vorticity-confinement term
(`f = eps * normalize(grad|curl|) x curl`, eps = 0.32) feeds energy back into the small eddies
that the advection numerically damps, which is what keeps the curls crisp instead of turning
to fog. Three stirrers orbit the tank on incommensurate Lissajous paths, each adding a
Gaussian blob of velocity rotating at its own rate and injecting its own palette hue; drag
and ink fade of 0.9955 per frame hold the tank at a steady density instead of saturating.
Being unconditionally stable, it cannot blow up however long it runs. Dense and soft-edged —
a ground layer. 166 fps, mean delta 0.94.
