# 186 — Marbling

Turkish ebru, run backwards. Marbling has exact closed-form maps and both are
inverted here per pixel rather than simulated. A DROP of radius r at c pushes the
whole sheet radially outward, P′ = c + (P−c)·√(1 + r²/|P−c|²), whose inverse is
|P| = √(|P′|²−r²) — and the disc of radius r has no preimage at all, which is
precisely why fresh ink lands as a clean round island that shoulders the older
pattern aside. A TINE (the comb stroke) is P′ = P + u·λ/(1+(z/β)²) where z is the
distance from the tine line; since a displacement parallel to the line leaves z
unchanged, its inverse is exact too. Four drops and three combs are inverted in
reverse order back onto a full-spectrum banded ground, so every pixel is one
short chain of algebra with no iteration and no error — the veins stay razor
sharp no matter how hard the sheet is combed. Drop centres, comb amplitudes and
the ground phase all creep on detuned slow sines, so the sheet flows like the
liquid it is imitating, and the ink islands carry concentric rings of their own.
Dense, full-bleed, high-chroma: this is a GROUND layer, the thing the sparse
routines are meant to sit on top of. 150 fps, delta 1.04.
