# 154 Hyperbolic Court

**Look.** A Poincare disk filled with a hyperbolic tiling — pentagons, heptagons or
octagons meeting in rosettes — whose cells shrink without limit toward the rim, drawn as
glowing seams over dark cells, the ring of infinity crowded with infinitely many
vanishing tiles. The whole tiling glides forever without repeating or restarting, and
the cell fills shift hue by reflection depth so the picture reads in coloured shells.
Circular figure on black: corners of the frame stay empty.

**Math.** Per pixel, the point is first carried by a hyperbolic translation
`z -> (z+a)/(1 + conj(a) z)` whose parameter `a` orbits slowly (this is an isometry of
the tiling, hence a seamless endless glide), then folded back into the Schwarz triangle
(2,p,q) by reflection: the two straight mirrors through the origin fold the angle into
the wedge pi/p, and the third mirror is the circle orthogonal to the unit circle with
`c = cos(pi/q)/sqrt(cos^2(pi/q) - sin^2(pi/p))`, `r = sqrt(c^2-1)`, applied by inversion;
iterate until the point stops moving (<=26 rounds). The Jacobian of the whole fold is
accumulated on the way (each inversion multiplies it by `r^2/|z-C|^2`), so the distance
to the nearest mirror can be divided back into *screen* units and the seams drawn at
constant width right up to the ideal boundary instead of aliasing into moire. Edge glow
is `1/(1+e^2)`, cells are filled faintly by reflection count, and a `1-|z|^2` fade kills
the last unresolvable ring. (p,q) is chosen per segment from six hyperbolic pairs.
400x300 canvas, upscaled. Measured 158-217 fps at 1280x960, delta 0.95-1.83.
