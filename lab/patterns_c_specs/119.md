# 119 — Rose Window

A Gothic rose window lit from behind: a central medallion of six foils, a ring of twelve large
foils, a ring of twenty-four small ones at the rim, radial mullions and four concentric stone
courses, every glass cell a different saturated hue, the stone near black. The window turns
once every twenty minutes and a slow wave of light travels outward through the glass as if
the sun were moving behind it. Each ring is tested by folding the pixel's angle into one
sector (`a = theta - round(theta/s)*s`) and applying the law of cosines,
`d^2 = r^2 + dp^2 - 2 r dp cos a`, with cos read from a 1024-entry table; comparing `d^2`
against `(rp +/- w)^2` keeps the entire window free of square roots, so foils, cusps,
mullions, courses and frame together cost about thirty operations per pixel on top of two
precomputed polar tables. Glass identity comes from which region the pixel falls in — inside
a large foil, inside a small foil, inside the medallion, or the field between — combined with
the sector index, which is why neighbouring petals differ in hue but each petal is one flat
colour. Full-bleed and saturated: a ground layer. 228 fps, mean delta 0.38.
