# 104 — Ice-Ray Lattice

A Chinese cracked-ice window (*bing lie*), generated the way the joiners cut them.
Take a hexagonal panel, find its longest edge and the longest edge not adjacent to
it, run a straight bar between a point on each, and recur on both halves until the
pieces are small — about seventy irregular convex cells, no two alike, none of them
ever crossing. Picking the cut edges by length rather than at random is the whole
trick: random picks produce slivers and fans, longest-to-longest produces cells with
the proportions the real lattices have. The tree of cuts is fixed once from a seed,
but the position of every cut rides its own slow sine (±0.13 of an edge, periods of
100–170 s), so the lattice keeps flexing — cells stretch, shear and trade
proportions while the joinery stays intact and the topology never changes, which is
why nothing ever pops. Bars are drawn as glowing strokes with hue running along each
bar and the panel turning ±6° and breathing ±4% in scale. Mean frame-to-frame delta
0.2–0.35, the calmest routine in the set, and ~86% near-black: pure line art, made to
be seen through.
