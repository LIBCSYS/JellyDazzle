# 121 — Chladni Sand

Sand figures on a vibrating rectangular plate. A standing wave on a plate with free
edges is the antisymmetric combination of two rectangular modes,
`f(u,v) = cos(n*pi*u)cos(m*pi*v) - cos(m*pi*u)cos(n*pi*v)`, and sand migrates off the
antinodes and piles up on the nodal set `f = 0` — the curves this routine draws.
Both mode numbers are kept *fractional* and glide on slow incommensurate sines
(n = 4.3 +/- 2.1, m = 7.6 +/- 3.1), so instead of snapping between the integer modes a
real plate would resonate at, the nodal curves stretch, pinch, split and re-lace
continuously. Brightness is `w/(w + f^2)` from a 1024-entry table, which puts a
2-3 px filament of light exactly on the zero set and leaves everything else black.
The symmetric companion field `g = cos(n*pi*u)cos(m*pi*v) + cos(m*pi*u)cos(n*pi*v)`
costs no extra multiplies (it is the sum where f is the difference of the same two
products) and drives the palette index, so hue crawls along each filament and the
curves read as iridescent sand rather than wire. Everything is separable: two
cosine tables over the columns, two over the rows, two multiplies and a table read
per pixel — no trig, no division, no square root in the inner loop, which is why it
runs full-resolution at ~670 fps. A slow zoom breath and a sub-pixel pan of the plate
origin keep the figure from ever sitting still. Overlay-qualified (86% near-black).
