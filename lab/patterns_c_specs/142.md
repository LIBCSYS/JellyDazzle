# 142 — Attractor Smoke

**Look.** A single luminous whorl of smoke hanging in black — thousands of hair-fine
filaments folded into shells and cusps, brightest where the folds pile up. It slowly
turns, and its shape reorganises: a lobe unrolls into a spiral, the spiral braids, the
braid closes back into a lobe. Sparse and centred, so it composites cleanly.

**Math.** The Peter de Jong map `x' = sin(a y) - cos(b x)`, `y' = sin(c x) - cos(d y)`.
6000 walkers advance 30 steps each per frame (180k samples) and bilinearly deposit into a
512x384 density field that decays 5% a frame. That decay is what makes it usable: the
picture is a *time-averaged exposure* over roughly 20 frames, so Monte-Carlo shot noise
cancels and the image has no flicker at all (measured frame-to-frame delta about 1.0).
The four coefficients sit on independent slow sinusoids around the classic values
(1.641, 1.902, 0.316, 1.525), amplitude around 0.3, periods 100–270 seconds, so the
attractor is continuously reshaped without ever cutting. 24 walkers are re-seeded per
frame so no basin can go permanently empty. Density is tone-mapped `1-exp(-kd)`, coloured
by the palette walked by density plus a diagonal gradient, and bilinearly upscaled. A slow
view rotation (0.00072 rad/frame) keeps the whole object turning.
