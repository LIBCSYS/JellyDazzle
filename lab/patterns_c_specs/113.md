# 113 — Sandpile Mandala

The abelian sandpile, growing on screen: a crystalline mandala of nested triangular domains
with dead-straight boundaries, unmistakably fractal, filling the frame over about two
minutes. Grains are dropped 190 at a time on the centre cell of a 385x385 lattice; every cell
holding four or more grains topples, handing one grain to each of its four neighbours, and
grains that fall off the lattice edge are lost (open boundary). Relaxation runs through a
toppling queue with an in-queue flag, so the cost is one visit per actual topple rather than
one sweep per cell, and the pile is fully relaxed in every rendered frame — the theorem the
model is named for says the result does not depend on the order. Heights 0-3 take four hues
from the live palette at rising brightness; a cell caught mid-avalanche is drawn hot. The
lattice is shown one cell per canvas pixel and never zoomed, which is the trick that keeps
the motion budget near zero: every edge that moves is one pixel wide. A twenty-frame temporal
average turns each cell's change of height into a soft melt rather than a flicker. 223 fps,
mean delta 0.19, worst 0.69 over 1500 frames.
