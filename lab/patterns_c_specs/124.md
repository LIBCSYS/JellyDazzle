# 124 — Poincare Web

The {4,q} hyperbolic tiling in the Poincare disk, drawn as its mirror lines. The
(2,4,q) triangle group has two straight mirrors 45 degrees apart plus one geodesic
mirror — the circle orthogonal to the unit circle with
`d = cos(pi/q)/sqrt(cos^2(pi/4) - sin^2(pi/q))` and `rc^2 = d^2 - 1`. Choosing p = 4
is what makes this cheap: the straight mirror pair is exactly the octant fold, so
folding a point into the fundamental triangle is `abs, abs, compare-and-swap` plus at
most 21 circle inversions, with no atan2 anywhere. Each inversion multiplies the local
derivative by `rc^2/|z-c|^2`; carrying that product lets the mirror distances be
converted back into *screen* units, so lines keep constant pixel width all the way out
instead of piling into a solid bright rim — the single detail that separates a
hyperbolic tiling that reads from one that does not. The three mirror distances are
summed (not min'd) so triangle vertices burn white. q itself glides between about 4.7
and 7.4, so the vertex figure breathes and the tiling continuously rebuilds; on top of
that a Mobius translation `(z+a)/(1+conj(a)z)` with |a| oscillating around 0.42 slides
the whole hyperbolic plane past the window, the one motion Euclidean tilings cannot
make. Iteration colour indexes the palette, giving concentric bands from the origin
outward. Overlay-qualified (69% near-black), and the disk leaves the frame corners
completely black for compositing.
