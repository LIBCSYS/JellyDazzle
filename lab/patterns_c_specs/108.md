# 108 — Vega Bulge

Vasarely's *Vega* series, done as light on black instead of ink on white. Three
invisible lenses drift under a ruled grid; each displaces the lattice radially by
`d = A/(1 + r²/R²)` applied to `(p − c)`, so the ruling stretches over a lens and
compresses at its rim and the eye insists on reading a sphere pushing through the
page. Two lenses bulge and one pinches. The falloff table is tapered to reach exactly
zero at its edge — a lens that merely gets small leaves a visible step in every line
that crosses its rim, and the taper is what removes it. Grid pitch (25 ±4 px), lens
strengths, lens radii and lens positions all breathe on independent slow sines, and
the whole ruling turns a few degrees, so the swells wander and swap without the grid
ever tearing. Line brightness rides the accumulated local stretch, which makes each
bulge glow at its crown, and hue walks with the warped coordinate so every swell
carries its own colour band. The inner loop is three table lookups and two multiplies
per pixel over a 480×360 field, then a bilinear upscale. ~75% near-black: lines only,
interiors empty, so it stacks over a ground without hiding it.
