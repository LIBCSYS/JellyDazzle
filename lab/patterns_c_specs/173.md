# 173 — Steiner Necklace

Nested rings of mutually tangent circles, pushed through a circle inversion. A Steiner
chain is the closed ring of n circles fitting between two given circles and touching each
other; in the concentric case the geometry is exact and needs no solver — centre distance
1, chain radius `sin(pi/n)`, boundaries at `1 +/- sin(pi/n)`. Steiner's porism guarantees
the chain can be rotated freely and stays closed, which is why the necklace can turn
forever without the tangencies drifting. Three chains are nested by using the inner
boundary of one as the outer boundary of the next (scale factor `(1-rho)/(1+rho)` per
level), each with its own pearl count (5-12) and counter-rotation. The whole nest is then
inverted in a circle whose centre drifts on a slow orbit **outside** the figure: inversion
maps circles to circles and preserves tangency, so the necklace stays exactly tangent
while going wildly eccentric — pearls crowd and shrink on one side, swell and idle on the
other. Framing is automatic and needs no clamps: the image of the outermost boundary
circle is computed first and the whole scene is scaled and translated so that one circle
always fills the frame. Each pearl is drawn as a rim with a one-pixel halo either side and
a spark at every tangency point, with the deposited energy normalised by arc length so a
2 px pearl is no brighter per unit line than a 200 px one. 91% near-black — the sparsest
routine here. Mean frame delta 1.3.
