# 132 Tesseract Drift

**Look.** A glowing wireframe hypercube tumbling on black, with a second, spikier solid
nested inside it. Because the rotation is happening in four dimensions, the cube does not
merely turn — it turns *inside out*: inner faces swell through outer ones, the small
central cube inflates until it becomes the big one and the big one collapses to a point,
and the whole cycle repeats without ever passing through a discontinuity. Edges are
coloured by depth in the fourth dimension, so a face keeps its colour as it everts and the
eye can follow it around. About 95% of the frame is black; it is a pure overlay.

**Math.** 16 vertices at `(+-0.62)^4` with the 32 edges that differ in one coordinate, plus
a 16-cell (cross-polytope: the 8 points `+-e_i`, all 24 non-antipodal pairs) counter-rotated
in the `yw` plane. Two simultaneous 4D plane rotations are applied, one in `xy` and one in
`zw` at incommensurate rates — the isoclinic turn that produces the eversion — followed by a
slow `xz` tilt so the solid never sits flat. Projection is two perspective divides in
series: `4D -> 3D` by `2.55/(2.55 - w)`, then `3D -> 2D` by `3.05/(3.05 - z)`, so edges near
the 4-viewer swell and brighten and far ones shrink into the core. Hue indexes the palette
on `0.26 w + 0.30 k3` (fourth-dimension depth plus the 3D perspective factor). Edges are
additively rasterised as filaments into a 384x288 float canvas at 1.6 samples/pixel, vertices
get a single bright splat, then a 3x3 bloom, exponential tone map and bilinear upscale.
Measured 369 fps at 1280x960, delta 1.42-1.73.
