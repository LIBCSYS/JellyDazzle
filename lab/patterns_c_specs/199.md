# 199 — Talbot Carpet

The self-image lattice behind a diffraction grating, computed the way the physics writes
it. Light leaving a periodic grating rebuilds a perfect copy of the grating at the Talbot
distance, a half-period-shifted copy at half of it, and rational fractions of the period
everywhere between — the field in the plane behind the grating is a genuine fractal. Take
the grating's Fourier coefficients `c_0 = d`, `c_n = sin(pi n d)/(pi n)` for duty cycle d,
turn each by the Fresnel phase `-pi lambda z n^2`, sum and square. Screen x is the grating
coordinate and screen y is distance downstream, so a full Talbot period is on show at
once. Two facts make it cheap: `c_n = c_-n` and the phase depends on n^2, so the n and -n
terms collapse to `2 c_n cos(2 pi n x) e^(i theta_n)`, and the spatial cosines depend only
on x, so they are tabulated once a frame for nine harmonics and the inner loop is nine
multiply-accumulate pairs with no trig at all. The carpet drifts downstream at about
0.0004 Talbot lengths a frame while the duty cycle breathes between 0.20 and 0.52, which
reshapes every branch of the lattice at once; exposure is normalised by the grating's own
mean intensity `d`, so the frame keeps its blacks whatever the duty does. Intensity drives
both brightness and hue, which is what makes the self-images read as white cores inside
coloured diamonds. 270 fps, mean frame delta 0.74, only 12-20% near-black — this is a
ground, and the starkest, most graphic thing in these ten.
