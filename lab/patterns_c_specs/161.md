# 161 — Curve Loom

A single unbroken wire that visits all 1024 cells of a 32x32 grid, morphing between
three different ways of visiting them. Three orderings are precomputed at init — the
order-5 **Hilbert** curve (the standard `d2xy` bit-rotation construction), a
**boustrophedon** snake, and an **inward spiral** — and each gives every index `i` a
position `C_k(i)` in `[-1,1]^2`. The drawn vertex is the weighted blend
`P(i) = sum_k w_k(t) C_k(i)` where the three weights are a cyclic, smoothstepped
triangular partition of unity in a phase that advances at 0.00055 rad/frame, so the
figure spends most of its time as one recognisable curve and crossfades through the
others; because the blend is per-vertex and the weights are C1, the wire deforms
rather than cutting. Mixed weights contract the figure toward its centroid, so the
blend is renormalised each frame by its RMS radius (`scale = 0.582/rms`) — that one
line is what keeps it filling the frame at every point of the morph. The polyline is
walked segment by segment and splatted at ~1.1 px spacing into a 480x360 float plate
with a 5-tap soft stroke (bilinear core plus a dim cross at +/-1.6 px), and the plate
decays 0.74/frame, which turns the stamps into a glowing filament with a little bloom
instead of a hairline. Brightness carries a travelling pulse in *index* space,
`0.42 + 0.58 cos^3`, phase-advanced 0.0011/frame, so light appears to run along the
wire's own arclength — the only cue that tells you it is one curve and not a bundle.
Hue walks with index and with time from the live palette ramp. Measured: ~250 fps at
1280x960, mean frame delta 0.47, 88% near-black — an overlay. The plate is
deliberately *not* cleared on an `sl` discontinuity: it is self-refreshing, and
clearing it would be a hard cut.
