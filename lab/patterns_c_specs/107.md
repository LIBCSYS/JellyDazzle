# 107 — Stereographic Cage

The icosahedral group has fifteen two-fold axes, and the great circles normal to them
cut the sphere into the 120 triangles of its full symmetry group. This routine tumbles
that sphere on three incommensurate rotations and projects it stereographically from
the north pole — the map `(x,y,z) → (x,y)/(1−z)`, which sends every great circle to a
circle or a straight line in the plane and preserves every angle exactly. The result
is a cage of enormous sweeping arcs: as a circle passes near the projection pole its
image swells toward infinity and then contracts again, so the figure is continuously
turning itself inside out while every intersection angle stays fixed at the values the
icosahedron dictates. Circles are sampled 420 times and stroked between consecutive
samples with a sub-step count set by the projected length, which keeps the line solid
and its brightness constant however far the projection stretches it; samples within
0.035 of the pole are dropped, so an arc fades off the frame instead of exploding.
The twelve icosahedron vertices ride along as eight-point glints, brightening as they
approach the pole. Per-circle hue from the live palette. ~85% near-black — the
emptiest routine here, and the only one whose marks are longer than the screen.
