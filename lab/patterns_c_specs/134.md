# 134 Aurora Curtain

**Look.** Seven sheets of green-to-violet light hanging over a star field, folding back on
themselves so the creases stack into bright vertical columns, scalloped along the top into
tall rays and cut off sharp along the bottom the way a real auroral arc is. The sheets
drift through one another and, because everything is additive, a fold crossing another
curtain flares instead of occluding. Motion is a slow luminous surge travelling along each
sheet rather than any flicker. Dark at the top and bottom (dark 0.59) but dense enough
through the middle band to carry a scene on its own.

**Math.** Each curtain is a ribbon standing vertically on a ground track that wanders in the
horizontal plane: `X(s)` and `Z(s)` are two slow low-order Fourier curves in the parameter
`s`, and the ribbon is drawn in perspective — screen `x = X/Z`, height proportional to
`1/Z`. Because the track curves toward and away from the viewer, the map `s -> x` is *not*
monotonic; the sheet folds back on itself and the folds pile light on light, which is
precisely how a real aurora gets its bright creases. Along the sheet a fine sinusoid in `s`
gives the ray striation, and the same striation also modulates the crown height, so bright
rays stand taller and the top edge is scalloped instead of a flat slab. Up the sheet, a
`v^1.35` ramp fades the intensity and the palette index climbs, so each curtain runs from a
dense base to a thin, differently-coloured crown; that gradient is precomputed once per
curtain into a 128-entry table so the inner loop is two multiply-adds per pixel. 1600
samples per curtain into a 384x288 float canvas, plus 210 slow-twinkling stars and a very
dark blue vertical sky wash. Measured 273 fps at 1280x960, delta 0.27-0.28.
