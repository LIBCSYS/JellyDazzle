# 172 — Villarceau Weave

The fourth family of circles on a torus. Cut a torus with a plane through its centre that
is tangent to it at two points and the section is not an oval but two exact circles,
tilted out of the equatorial plane by `asin(r/R)`. The base circle is
`p(t) = (r + R cos t, sqrt(R^2 - r^2) sin t, r sin t)`, which was verified algebraically
against the torus equation before a line was drawn: `sqrt(px^2+py^2) = R + r cos t`, so
`(sqrt(px^2+py^2) - R)^2 + pz^2 = r^2 cos^2 t + r^2 sin^2 t = r^2`, exactly. Twenty-two
copies of it are spun about the axis, then the whole family is mirrored in z to get the
opposite chirality and offset half a step, so the two oblique families cross everywhere
and the torus appears **woven** out of rings that are each individually flat. The tube
radius breathes on a 0.00073 rad/frame sine, the two families counter-slide around the
axis, and the object precesses rather than tumbles — pitch and yaw are bounded sines and
the only unbounded rotation is a slow roll applied in screen space after projection, which
is what keeps a figure this wide from ever leaving the frame. Brightness is
`0.34 + 1.7/den^2` with a travelling pulse along each ring, so near rings read hot and far
rings sink into the black. 81% near-black, mean frame delta 0.8.
