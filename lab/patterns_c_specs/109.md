# 109 — Rain Pond

An actual wave equation, raindrop by raindrop. A 320×240 height field is stepped with
the discrete scheme `u' = 2u − u_prev + C·∇²u` at `C = 0.30` (comfortably inside the
stability limit) and damped 0.45% a frame, with fixed edges so the rim of the pond
reflects. New state is written into the old previous-state buffer and the two swap
roles, so the Laplacian always reads one clean snapshot — an in-place update biases
the stencil sideways and the rings come out lopsided. Drops land on a 55–175 frame
clock, each one a Gaussian dent poured in over eight frames so nothing pops. Because
the state is genuinely integrated, the rings do what real rings do: spread at
constant speed, pass through each other into moiré rosettes, reflect off the rim and
come back. Lighting is a low sun: the shading term is the surface slope dotted with a
light vector that turns once every two minutes, raised to the fourth power, which
paints thin blazing crest lines and leaves the troughs at the near-black water
colour, plus a gradient-magnitude term that gives the steepest wavefronts a rim. Hue
tracks height and slope, so crests and troughs sit in different parts of the scheme.
58–85% near-black depending on how recently it has rained.
