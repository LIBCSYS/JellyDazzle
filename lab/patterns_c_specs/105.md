# 105 — Rule Mandala

Wolfram's elementary cellular automaton, wrapped around a circle instead of stacked
down a page. A 256-cell ring with periodic boundaries steps every 5 frames; each new
generation is laid down at the centre and the older ones are pushed outward, so the
automaton's space-time diagram becomes a mandala growing out of its own middle.
Sierpinski gaskets curl into rosettes, rule 22's triangles fan into petals, rule 150
builds interlocking diamond lattices. Only the left–right symmetric rules are used
(90, 150, 18, 22, 126, 182) over a mirrored seed, which guarantees a bilaterally
symmetric figure; the rule changes every 96 generations with a fresh three-cell seed,
and because the older rings are never rewritten the change arrives as a new texture
growing out of the centre rather than as a cut. Radii advance by a fifth of the ring
spacing per frame (the fractional part of the generation clock is carried into the
radius), so the outward crawl is continuous rather than stepped, and the whole
mandala turns slowly. Live cells only — dead cells are black — with the newest three
rings brightened to mark the growth front and hue banding outward by generation.
76–98% near-black.
