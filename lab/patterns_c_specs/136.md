# 136 Dipole Lines

**Look.** The physics-textbook plate, animated: hundreds of fine curved filaments streaming
out of three bright sources, arcing across black and diving into three sinks, with the
familiar bulge-and-pinch where two like poles push their fields apart. The charges drift on
slow orbits, so the whole flux map breathes — lobes swell, a bridge between two poles
narrows and snaps, arcs that reached off-frame curl back and find a sink. Each filament is
colour-graded along its own length. It is the sparsest, darkest frame in the batch
(luma 4.6): almost pure line on almost pure black.

**Math.** Six point charges of alternating sign on slow, mutually detuned Lissajous orbits.
From every positive charge 44 seeds are released on a small circle and integrated along the
true 2D field `E = sum q (p - c)/|p - c|^2` with a fixed arc-length step of 1.15 px, giving
equal-flux streamlines: they crowd where the field is strong and splay where it is weak,
which is the property that makes field-line pictures readable. A line stops when it comes
within ~5 px of a negative charge — without that test it stalls at the sink and stacks
hundreds of splats on one pixel, which showed up as a white square in the first build. Hue
indexes the palette on `log|E|^2`, so each filament shades continuously from its hot origin
to its cool far field. Brightness decays with arc length, which does double duty: it gives
the lines their falloff, and it means that when the drifting charges cross a separatrix and a
line re-attaches to a different sink, only the dim far end of that line moves — the topology
change is invisible, and the measured delta stays at 0.29. 384x288 float canvas, additive
bilinear splats, 3x3 bloom, bilinear upscale. Measured 338 fps at 1280x960.
