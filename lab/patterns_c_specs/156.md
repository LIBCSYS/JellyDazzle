# 156 Apollonian Tide

**Look.** An Apollonian gasket — three big circles inside a rim, the gaps packed with
ever-smaller tangent circles down to specks — drawn as thin luminous rings on black,
with hue keyed to size (rim one colour, mid circles another, the dust a third). It is
never still: the packing swells on one side and compresses on the other as an inversion
sweeps around it, and a wave of brightness climbs the scale ladder from the biggest
circles to the smallest.

**Math.** The packing comes from Descartes' circle theorem in its linear (Lagarias)
form: for four mutually tangent circles the two solutions for the fourth satisfy
`(k, k z)_new = 2 (k1+k2+k3, k1 z1 + k2 z2 + k3 z3) - (k0, k0 z0)`, so each new circle is
one subtraction on the triple (curvature, curvature x centre). Starting from the rim
(k = -1) and three equal circles of curvature `1/(2 sqrt3 - 3)`, the recursion is walked
on an explicit stack until the radius falls under ~0.008, giving up to 2400 circles,
cached per segment. Each frame every circle is pushed through a circle inversion whose
centre orbits outside the rim: inversion maps circles to circles exactly, with
`s = rho^2/(|C-P|^2 - r^2)`, `C' = P + s(C-P)`, `r' = |s| r` — so the gasket stays a
gasket while its scale distribution slides. The frame is re-normalised on the image of
the rim so the composition never drifts off screen. Rings are drawn by splatting points
around each circle at ~7 samples/px of radius, weighted by a `log r` brightness wave,
blurred, tone-mapped, upscaled from 480x360. Measured 272-324 fps, delta 0.88-1.31.
