# 128 — Aggregate Bloom

Diffusion-limited aggregation, grown twelve ways. A genuine DLA cluster is built once
at init on a 321x321 lattice: walkers are released just outside the current cluster
radius, take long jumps while far from it (the standard first-passage speedup) and
single lattice steps once close, and freeze where they first touch. Stick-on-contact
is what produces screening — outer tips intercept nearly every walker — so the result
is a branched dendrite of fractal dimension near 1.7, not a disc. Crucially, sticking
is *rejected outside a 28-degree wedge*, so what grows is a single arm rather than a
blob; the arm is then stamped through six rotations and a mirror, and the twelve
copies interlock into a snowflake with real black between them instead of overlapping
into grain (the version without the wedge measured 50% near-black and looked like
static; with it, 94%). Playback is keyed to the segment clock `sl`: particles appear
in birth order over the first 1400 frames with a smoothstep ease, the growing tips
running hotter and whiter than the settled interior, and the whole bloom dissolves
over the last 500 frames so the 2048-frame loop has no seam. Each particle is drawn
as a five-tap bead so the dendrite stays connected at the 1.47x render scale. Slow
rotation and a 3% scale breath. ~440 fps, delta 0.15.
