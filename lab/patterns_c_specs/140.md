# 140 Newton Filigree

**Look.** A three-armed lace of glowing hairlines on pure black, every strand fringed with
smaller copies of itself down to the resolution limit, meeting at brilliant star-points
where three arms cross. The whole figure turns, breathes in scale, and spirals: strands
that were straight coil up, the lace tightens and relaxes. Iridescent — each strand is
stained by the two basins it separates, so the colour changes along its length. ~90%
near-black, the classic overlay.

**Math.** Every pixel is a starting point for twelve steps of a *relaxed* Newton iteration
on `z^3 - 1`, written in the closed form `z <- z(1 - a/3) + (a/3)/z^2` so one complex
reciprocal-square is the whole step. The relaxation constant `a` is complex and crawls
around a circle of radius 0.195 centred on 1 — large enough to spiral and deform the fractal
continuously, small enough that basin interiors still converge to the floating-point floor.
Interior points converge quadratically; points on the basin boundary — the Julia set of the
Newton map — never converge, and their residual `|z^3 - 1|` stays order one. Brightness is
therefore `r^(1/k)`, which collapses that dynamic range into a filament exactly on the
boundary. Single precision bottoms the residual out near `2^-24`, so the usable range of
`log2 r` is only 0 to -24 and the exponent has to be steep (0.50) or every interior stays
lit — that was the whole tuning problem. `log2` is read straight off the IEEE exponent field
with a linear mantissa correction, and the profile is a 512-entry table, so the per-pixel
tail after the iteration is two table lookups. Hue comes from `arg(z_final)` via a
polynomial `atan2`, so each of the three basins stains its own side of the lace. 320x240
canvas — the heaviest of the ten at 137 fps — 3x3 bloom, bilinear upscale. Delta 0.90-1.01.
