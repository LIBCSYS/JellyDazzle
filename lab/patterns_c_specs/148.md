# 148 — Pendulum Wave

**Look.** The physics-lecture kinetic sculpture, mirrored top and bottom into a
chandelier. Thirty bobs on wires swing in and out of phase, ribbons of light trailing
behind them; they braid into a travelling wave, split into two counter-running waves, then
four, dissolve into apparent chaos, and re-converge — a full cycle every two minutes,
exactly periodic.

**Math.** Pendulum `i` is tuned to make exactly `K+i` swings per cycle (`K = 18`,
`T = 7200` frames), so `w_i = 2 pi (K+i)/T`. The drawn length runs as `sqrt(K/(K+i))`
rather than the physical `1/w^2`, because the true ladder collapses the fast end into the
rail and ruins the composition; frequencies stay exact, so the wave behaviour is the real
thing. Swing amplitude breathes around 0.85 rad. Each bob is stamped three times per frame
at 1/3-frame intervals so the ribbon is continuous rather than dashed, using a 19x19
Gaussian kernel; the wire is 34 bilinear points fading toward the pivot. Everything lands
in a 640x480 float canvas that decays 4.5% per frame (about a 22-frame memory), tone-mapped
`a/(a+300)` and bilinearly upscaled. Bob colour is the palette at 780 index units per
pendulum, brightness-normalised so bobs stay vivid in dark schemes.
