# 103 — Dragon Unfold

The Heighway dragon caught in the middle of being folded. Fold a strip of paper in
half thirteen times the same way and open every crease to the same angle: the crease
directions are the paperfolding sequence, `turn(i) = ((i & −i) << 1) & i`, computed
once for 8192 segments. Nothing about the curve's combinatorics animates — only the
angle each crease opens to, which glides between 54° and 90° on a 58-second sine. At
90° it is the familiar dragon fractal that tiles the plane; below that it relaxes
through scrolls, ribbons and spirals that have no names, and it passes through all of
them without ever being cut or redrawn. A second copy rotated a half turn about the
shared origin closes the figure into the twindragon rosette. Two things keep it
watchable: the frame fit is lagged (the scale chases the bounding box at 4.5% a
frame, so the figure breathes instead of jumping), and exposure is automatic — as the
creases close, the curve folds onto itself and ink per pixel swings by two orders of
magnitude, so a lagged gain driven by the accumulator's mean holds the picture at a
constant density with no visible pumping. Hue runs along arclength in blocks of 32
segments, which colours each self-similar lobe separately. 78–96% near-black.
