# 194 — Harmonic Shell

A wire sphere breathing in spherical-harmonic modes. The mesh radius is
`r(theta,phi) = 1 + a (w1 Y1 + w2 Y2)` where each Y is a real spherical harmonic
`P_l^m(cos theta) cos(m phi)`, the associated Legendre value coming from the standard
three-term recurrence and normalised to unit peak at build time. Because P depends only
on latitude and `cos(m phi)` only on longitude, the entire 72x40 mesh is one outer product
per mode — which is why a full harmonic solid costs less per frame than a plasma. Two
modes are held at once and cross-faded on a 4.7-minute cosine; the instant one reaches
zero weight it is quietly swapped for another (l,m) from a table of fourteen, from (2,0)
up to (7,6), so the solid keeps arriving at shapes it has not shown before and never
cycles. Amplitude breathes between 0.17 and 0.43, the solid yaws steadily and nods on a
slow sine. Drawn as latitude rings and meridians only, additively, with far-side vertices
at 34% weight — so the back of the shell glows faintly through the front and the thing
reads as blown glass rather than as a surface. Hue tracks the radial deviation, so lobes
and dimples take opposite ends of the palette window. 268 fps, mean frame delta 0.31,
77% near-black — overlay.
