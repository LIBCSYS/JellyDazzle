# 168 — Schotter Drift

Georg Nees' 1968 plotter piece *Schotter* ("gravel") — arguably the first famous work
of generative art — set in motion. A 15x13 grid of squares is perfectly ruled at the
top and progressively loses its discipline downward: cell `(i,j)` is rotated by
`1.35 D` radians and displaced by `11 D` pixels in each axis, where the disorder
`D = (v - 0.22 W(t))^1.75 * A(t)` grows as a power law in the normalised row `v`. Nees
drew his disorder from a random number generator, which is why his squares jitter;
here each cell owns three fixed phases and three fixed angular rates (0.0045-0.012
rad/frame, assigned once from the seed) and its rotation and both offsets are *slow
sinusoids* of those, so the gravel wanders continuously and no square ever moves more
than a fraction of a pixel per frame. Two slow envelopes do the composition: `A(t)`
breathes the overall amount of chaos between 0.24 and 1.0, and `W(t)` slides the
order/chaos front up and down the grid, so the piece keeps re-forming into a ruled
lattice and dissolving again. Squares are drawn as outlines only — four segments each,
stamped with the 5-tap stroke into a plate decaying 0.72/frame for a slight neon bloom
— and the most disordered cells are dimmed 30%, which reads as depth. Hue advances 6
entries per column and 3 per row, so the grid is a smooth two-axis colour field rather
than 195 unrelated colours. Measured: ~250 fps at 1280x960, mean frame delta 0.25, 78%
near-black — an overlay, and the most graphic/op-art item in this set, which is the
"stark" end of the range J asked for.
