# 169 — Braid Loom

Seven strands plaiting over and under each other — a real braid, not a bundle of
curves. Every strand is the same sinusoid at its own phase,
`y_i(x) = cy(x) + A sin(k x + w t + 2 pi i / 7)`, and the crucial line is that its
**depth is the cosine of the same phase**, `z_i = cos(...)`: a strand at the top of its
swing is at one extreme of depth and at the bottom is at the other, which is precisely
the phase relationship that makes a plait. The image is painted column by column; in
each column the seven strands are insertion-sorted by `z` and drawn far-to-near, and
each strand first lays down a 2.6 px black rim before its own shading, so the near
strand cuts a clean dark gap in the far one and the crossings read as over-under
instead of as a pile. Shading is a tube cross-section `1 - (dy/hw)^2` scaled by a depth
term `0.52 + 0.48 (1+z)/2` (far strands darker) and a specular band that travels along
the braid at 0.9 px/frame. The band's centre line `cy(x)` is itself two slow sines, so
the whole rope snakes across the frame and slowly changes its lay. This is the one
pattern in the set with no accumulator: it is a painter's-algorithm repaint into the
low-res image, `memset` then spans, which is why occlusion works at all — an additive
plate cannot erase. Off the band the frame is black, so despite being a solid,
saturated object it is still 83% near-black. Measured: ~270 fps at 1280x960, mean frame
delta 1.16. The obvious stacking use is as a bright MAX overlay over a dark field.
