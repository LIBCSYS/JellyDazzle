# 122 — Caustic Net

The web of light on the floor of a swimming pool. A water surface `h(x,y,t)` is built
from five slow plane waves (wavelengths 131-437 px, drifting directions, four
different phase velocities). Refracted rays land at `p + k*grad h`, a map whose
Jacobian is `I + k*Hess(h)`, and conservation of energy makes floor brightness
`1/|det J|` — which blows up along the curves where the Jacobian is singular and
neighbouring rays cross. Those fold curves are the caustic net, and this routine
draws them directly from the determinant rather than by tracing photons. The three
second derivatives of a plane wave are all the same sine times a constant, so each
wave costs exactly one table read: the inner loop is five reads, fifteen
multiply-adds, one 2x2 determinant and one reciprocal. Amplitudes are set so the
determinant genuinely crosses zero — below that threshold the effect degenerates into
soft blobs. Hue comes from two sources multiplied together: the surface height itself
(fast, so colour swims with the swell) and a slowly rotating large-scale gradient
(so different parts of the pool sit in different regions of the palette at once).
Rendered at 640x480 and bilinear-upscaled; the blur that costs is the blur that keeps
the frame-to-frame delta at 0.21. Overlay-qualified (67% near-black).
