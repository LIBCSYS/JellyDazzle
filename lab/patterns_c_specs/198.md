# 198 — Whirl Nest

The schoolbook whirl, doubled and set spinning. Take a regular n-gon and build the next
one by walking a fraction f along every edge; repeat, and each polygon is the previous one
shrunk by `sqrt(1 - 2f(1-f)(1-cos(2pi/n)))` and rotated by a fixed angle, so the corners
trace a logarithmic spiral and sixty-two levels of outline make the classic whirl. This
draws two of them at once, f and 1-f, which are the mirror-image chiralities of the same
nest: the two spiral families cross at every level and the frame reads as a woven star
rather than a single vortex. n is diced from 3 to 11 per segment, f breathes between about
0.055 and 0.13 on a 2.5-minute sine — which changes the pitch of both spiral families
together, so the whole nest winds and unwinds — and the two whirls counter-rotate. Hue
runs from the outer ring to the centre across the palette window, and a brightness pulse
travels inward level by level in opposite directions for the two chiralities, so something
is always moving even though the geometry is nearly static. Nothing but hairlines: 62
levels times n edges times two, drawn energy-conserving so line brightness does not depend
on how short the innermost edges get. 250 fps, mean frame delta 0.27, 60% near-black —
the densest of these ten and the one that will most often want slot 0.
