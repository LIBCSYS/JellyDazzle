# 150 — Relief Lantern

**Look.** A carved metal panel — deep radial flutes, a twelve-fold rosette, a boss
lattice and fine filigree ridging — lit by three coloured lamps that drift slowly across
it. Every ridge has a lit face and a shadowed face, so the image is high-contrast and
mostly one hue plus lamp colour: the "stark" end of the range, and a natural ground layer
under the sparse patterns.

**Math.** A height field is built once at full resolution from six terms (flutes
`sin(22 r/M)`, rosette `cos(12 th) exp(-1.6 r/M)`, twisted spokes `cos(6 th + 10 r/M)`,
boss lattice `sin(36x/M) sin(36y/M)`, filigree `sin(30 th + 48 r/M)`, fine rings
`sin(154 r/M)`) and reduced to an int8 normal map plus an ambient-occlusion byte. After
that the geometry never moves: the entire animation is three point lamps on Lissajous
paths (about 1 px/frame), each contributing a *signed* emboss term `74 + (N.L)` — signed
so ridges get both a highlight and a shadow — times a smooth `(1-d)^2/(1+7d)` distance
falloff that reaches exactly zero at its radius, so no lamp leaves a hard circular edge.
Relief depth breathes 25%, which costs nothing because it scales the light vector rather
than the map. Lamp colours are taken 9700 palette units apart for real chromatic
separation; measured delta 0.35 at full resolution and full brightness.
