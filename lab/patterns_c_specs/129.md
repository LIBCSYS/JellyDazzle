# 129 — Torus Knot

A glowing (p,q) torus knot tumbling in three dimensions — the only true 3-D wireframe
in the library. The curve is `x = (R + a cos qt) cos pt`, `y = (R + a cos qt) sin pt`,
`z = a sin qt`, traced with 3000 samples, rotated by two slowly precessing Euler
angles and projected through a real perspective divide (focal 3.4). Each sample is
stamped as a soft bead whose radius (0.4-3.4 px) and weight both scale with the depth
term, so the strand reads as a tube passing in front of and behind itself: the near
pass is fat and hot, the far pass thin and dim, and every crossing is unambiguous
without a depth sort — additive blending makes ordering irrelevant. (p,q) is drawn
from the segment seed out of eight coprime pairs (2:3, 3:2, 2:5, 5:2, 3:4, 4:3, 3:5,
2:7), which are genuinely different knots, so consecutive segments never show the same
one; inside a segment the tube radius a and the tumble keep morphing. Hue advances
with arclength, so the knot is a continuous ribbon through the palette and you can
follow the strand by colour through its own crossings. 97% near-black and only ~3
mean luma — the most transparent routine in the set, designed to be laid over anything
at high weight without touching the ground beneath it. ~430 fps, delta 0.10.
