# 139 Harmonic Orb

**Look.** Two shaded spheres on black — one large, one small — with coloured lobes painted
across them and bright white wires threading between the lobes like seams on a ball. The
spheres turn on two axes and the pattern on them morphs at a different rate again, sliding
between stacked latitude bands, a chequered lattice and orange-peel segments without ever
cutting between them. Lobes take opposite sides of the palette according to sign, so warm
and cool regions alternate across each seam, and a Fresnel rim keeps the silhouettes
luminous. The only shaded volume in this batch, and a useful counterweight to nine
line-art layers.

**Math.** Orthographic spheres, so the surface normal is just `(x, y, sqrt(1 - x^2 - y^2))` —
no ray-sphere intersection needed — rotated into body coordinates by a yaw and a pitch. On
each, a real spherical harmonic is evaluated directly as a Cartesian polynomial in that
normal, avoiding both Legendre recursion and `atan2`: `Y30 ~ nz(5nz^2 - 3)`,
`Y32 ~ (nx^2 - ny^2) nz`, `Y33 ~ nx(nx^2 - 3ny^2)`, `Y40 ~ 35nz^4 - 30nz^2 + 3`, mixed with
four slow detuned weights renormalised to sum to one. Because the mix is continuous, the
banding migrates between zonal, tesseral and sectoral forms rather than switching modes.
The surface is then read three ways at once: hue indexes the palette on signed `Y`
(`+-0.46` of the ramp); `|Y|^0.42` gives the lobes their body; and a narrow Gaussian
`exp(-620 Y^2)` lights the *nodal set* — the curves where the harmonic vanishes — as bright
wires laid over the shading, tinted in the palette's own colour so they do not bleach the
lobes they separate. A Lambert key light plus fill, a cubed Fresnel rim, and a
`(1 - r^2) R` ramp for silhouette antialiasing complete it. 384x288 canvas, bilinear
upscale. Measured 294 fps at 1280x960, delta 0.16.
