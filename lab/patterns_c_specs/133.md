# 133 Doyle Coil

**Look.** Circles of every size, each touching its neighbours, spiralling out from a tiny
bright knot at the centre to circles that overflow the frame. Nothing overlaps and nothing
is arbitrary — it reads immediately as a mechanism rather than a decoration. Over minutes
the coil breathes: the spiral pitch opens and closes, the size ratio between one turn and
the next slides, and the whole packing rotates, but every tangency holds throughout, so it
looks like a physical linkage being adjusted rather than an animation being played. Hue is
banded by generation, with a slow wave lighting the generations in turn. ~90% near-black.

**Math.** A hexagonal circle packing bent onto a logarithmic spiral. Put `q` circles in a
ring at radius `R`, each of radius `sR`: they touch each other iff `s = sin(pi/q)`. Advance
to the next ring by rotating `phi` and scaling by `lambda`; ring-to-ring tangency then
requires `1 + lambda^2 - 2 lambda cos(phi) = s^2 (1 + lambda)^2`, a quadratic whose closed
form is `lambda = (B + sqrt(B^2 - A^2))/A` with `A = 1 - s^2`, `B = cos(phi) + s^2`. So the
entire packing — every centre, every radius, to any depth — follows from the single free
parameter `phi`, and `phi` is what gets animated. Its admissible range works out to exactly
`(0, 2 pi/q)`; past *half* of that the nearest neighbour in the next ring switches sides and
the circles begin to overlap, so the sweep is capped just under 0.5 and the packing stays
legal for the whole cycle. `q` is 7..11 from the segment seed. The ring index range is
derived each frame from the visible size band with smooth fades at both ends, so nothing
pops in or out. Rims only, 7.2 samples per pixel of circumference, hue from `log R`.
Measured 374 fps at 1280x960, delta 0.07-0.15.
