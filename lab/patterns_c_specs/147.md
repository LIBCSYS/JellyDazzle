# 147 — Tesseract Drift

**Look.** Three nested neon wireframe hypercubes on black, counter-rotating inside one
another, edges changing hue along their length and swelling as they swing toward the
viewer. Because the rotation is genuinely four-dimensional, the solids turn inside out —
inner cube becomes outer cube — rather than merely spinning.

**Math.** The 16 vertices `(±1,±1,±1,±1)` are rotated in the xw, yz, xy and zw planes at
four incommensurate rates (0.001–0.004 rad/frame), perspective-projected 4D->3D with eye
distance 2.85 and 3D->2D with eye distance 1.55. The 32 edges (vertex pairs differing in
one bit) are stroked at two samples per pixel with a bilinear splat plus four
`0.3`-weight offsets at ±1.3 px, giving a soft three-pixel glow line. Palette index
follows the *interpolated w coordinate*, so an edge visibly changes colour as it passes
through the fourth dimension; brightness follows the product of both perspective factors,
so near edges bloom and far ones recede. Vertices get a 5x5 inverse-square bloom. Three
copies at 1.00 / 0.46 / 0.20 scale run different rotation rate sets and different palette
offsets.
