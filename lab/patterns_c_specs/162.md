# 162 — Orbital Lobes

The probability cloud of a hydrogen-like orbital, drawn as light. The field is the
separable product `psi(r,theta) = R(r) * Y(theta)`, with the radial part
`R(r) = r^l e^(-n r) sin(k pi r + phi)` — the `r^l` centrifugal rise, the exponential
tail, and a sine that supplies the radial nodes — and the angular part
`Y(theta) = cos(m theta)`. The whole trick that makes it animate without seams is how
`m` is allowed to be fractional: `cos(m theta)` with non-integer `m` is discontinuous
at `theta = 0`, so instead the angular table crossfades amplitude between the two
neighbouring integer lobe counts, `(1-f) cos(m0 theta) + f cos((m0+1) theta)`, which is
continuous everywhere and reads as one petal fading in between two others. Two such
orbitals with independent `l, n, k, m` and counter-rotating spins are superposed at a
drifting weight, and the displayed value is `|psi|^2` lifted by a gamma of 0.5. Cost is
kept low by baking the geometry once: per pixel the routine stores `r*1024` and
`theta/2pi*1024` as integers at init, so a frame is two table reads (a 1024-entry
radial LUT and a 1024-entry angular LUT, both rebuilt per frame in 2K trig calls
total), two multiplies and a glow LUT — no per-pixel trig at all, and the slow zoom is
just an integer rescale of the radial index. Positive and negative phase are tinted 88
ramp entries apart, which is what produces the two-colour lobes of a textbook
wavefunction, with the nodal surfaces reading as black seams between them. Measured:
~240 fps at 1280x960, mean frame delta 0.14 (one of the calmest in the library), 66%
near-black — overlay-qualified, and its dark nodal web makes it a good MAX layer over
a busier ground.
