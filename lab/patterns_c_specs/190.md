# 190 — Glenz Vectors

The Amiga demo trick that predates alpha blending. Two dual solids — a cube and
an octahedron — counter-rotate through each other on detuned axes, and every face
of both is filled ADDITIVELY: no z-buffer, no polygon sorting, no hidden-surface
removal at all. That absence *is* the effect. Because light adds, the far faces
show through the near ones and every region of overlap becomes its own brighter
colour, so a six-face cube reads as dozens of stained-glass cells whose
boundaries are the silhouettes of faces you cannot otherwise see. Face alpha
follows the projected area, i.e. |n·z| after rotation, so a face turning edge-on
fades out instead of collapsing into a hard line, and the twenty-four edges are
stroked over the top as filament highlights with depth-graded weight. Convex
spans are scanline-filled with fractional end-pixel coverage so the silhouettes
stay smooth while the solids turn, and a chroma boost at tone-map time pushes back
the desaturation that additive stacking always causes. One glowing jewel on black,
~75% of the frame at zero. 215 fps, delta 0.38.
