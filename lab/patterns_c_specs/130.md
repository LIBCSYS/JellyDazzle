# 130 — Pentagrid

De Bruijn's pentagrid, drawn as light instead of tiles. Five families of parallel
lines at 36-degree steps, each family taking a different hue from the palette, summed
additively. The five-fold arrangement is the dual construction of a Penrose tiling:
because tan(36 degrees) is irrational with respect to the grid spacings, the crossing
pattern is aperiodic and never repeats — it wanders forever without settling into a
lattice, which is exactly what keeps a line pattern from reading as wallpaper. Every
family's offset gamma_i creeps at its own rate (0.00055 to 0.00118 per frame) and the
whole grid turns about one hundred-thousandth of a turn per frame, so the vertices
drift like slow traffic and triple crossings — where three of the five families meet —
flare white. Each family renders through a 32-bit phase accumulator: one add, one
shift and one table read per family per pixel, with an early-out that skips the colour
multiply for the ~90% of pixels a given family is dark at, so the whole thing runs
full-resolution at ~320 fps with no trig and no division in the inner loop. Line
profile is `1/(1+(74e)^2)` in period units, which keeps constant relative width as the
spacing breathes between h/4.7 and h/6.5. Overlay-qualified (68% near-black); the
crossings are the brightest thing in it and everything else is void.
