# 164 — Kolam Loom

The single unbroken stroke of a South-Indian pulli kolam (the same construction as an
Angolan sona sand drawing): a ball launched at 45 degrees inside a rectangle traces one
closed path that threads around every dot of a lattice. In closed form that billiard
path is a Lissajous figure built from **triangle** waves rather than sines,
`x = Rx T(a s), y = Ry T(b s + phi)`, where `T` is a 1024-entry wave table that
interpolates between a pure sine and an exact triangle `asin(sin)*2/pi` at a blend
`k = 0.90 +/- 0.09` — near-triangle, so the stroke runs in straight diagonals with
softly rounded corners, which is exactly what rice flour does under a moving hand. The
wave counts `a` and `b` drift continuously on slow incommensurate sines in [4,6.6] and
[5,8]; at non-integer counts the loop does not close and instead precesses, so the
figure keeps re-weaving itself into a new lattice without a single frame of redraw.
6000 samples are stamped per frame into a decaying 480x360 plate (decay 0.80) with the
5-tap soft stroke, and a bead of light with an eighth-power cosine profile runs along
the parameter at 0.00055 turn/frame — the hand drawing the kolam. Underneath, a faint
9x7 pulli lattice is stamped at a fixed spacing tied to the stroke's own amplitudes, so
the loop visibly weaves around the dots. The whole figure carries a slow +/-0.2 rad
sway and a 3.5% breath. Measured: ~250 fps at 1280x960, mean frame delta 0.59, 89%
near-black — a sparse line-art overlay.
