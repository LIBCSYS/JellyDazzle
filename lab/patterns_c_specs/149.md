# 149 — Bubble Iris

**Look.** Nine soap bubbles drifting slowly across a near-black ground, each with a
transparent dark centre, a tight rainbow of interference bands crowded at the rim, a small
specular window highlight, and colour bands that creep downward as the film drains. They
overlap without turning to mush.

**Math.** Thin-film interference done properly enough to look right: a ray entering a
spherical shell at normalised radius `u` traverses `1/sqrt(1-u^2)` times the film
thickness, so the interference order — and therefore the palette index — diverges toward
the rim. That factor is a 1024-entry table indexed by `u^2`, clamped at 9. Film thickness
carries a vertical drain gradient `d0 (1 + drain * dy/R)` with `drain` about 0.55, and
`d0` wanders on a slow sinusoid per bubble, walking each film through the whole
interference sequence. Fresnel is `0.09 + 0.91 u^6`, which keeps centres nearly
transparent (the reason nine overlapping bubbles still read as nine bubbles) and puts all
the brightness at the rim; a twelve-unit ramp in `1-u^2` softens the outer edge. Bubble
centres follow independent Lissajous drifts at well under a pixel per frame, radii breathe
5%, and each bubble uses a different index scale so no two show the same band spacing.
