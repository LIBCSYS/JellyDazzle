# 120 — Rule Loom

A bolt of woven cloth creeping downward: nested Sierpinski triangles, woven diagonals and
combs in rainbow thread, the weave changing character in horizontal bands as older cloth
passes below. A 512-cell toroidal row is stepped by a Wolfram elementary rule — the eight bits
of the rule number indexed by the 3-cell neighbourhood — and 512 rows of history are kept as a
ring buffer. The loom advances one row every eighteen frames and the stack is drawn at a
fractional offset, the row shown at scanline y having age `y + 1 - phase`; that `+1 - phase`
is exactly what makes the step of the loom and the sub-row scroll cancel, and getting the sign
wrong cost 13 units of frame delta before the harness caught it. Every rule in the cycle
(90, 150, 18, 22, 60, 26, 146, 182) maps the empty neighbourhood to 0: a rule that does not —
105, for instance — inverts the whole row each generation, and a cloth of alternating rows
blows the motion budget the moment it scrolls. The rule changes every 90 rows and only affects
rows entering at the top, so the cloth below is undisturbed. Live cells take a hue drifting
along both row and column, dead cells a dark version of the same. Dense: a ground layer.
293 fps, mean delta 0.59.
