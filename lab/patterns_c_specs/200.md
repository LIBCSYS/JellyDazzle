# 200 — Lorenz Ribbon

The butterfly, drawn as a ribbon with real width. A fourth-order Runge-Kutta integration
of the Lorenz system (sigma 10, rho 28, beta 8/3, dt 0.0042) runs continuously into a ring
buffer of ten thousand states, prefilled at startup after 900 steps of burn-in so the
trajectory is already on the attractor when the first frame draws. Each frame advances the
integration only eight steps, so the curve slides forward along itself instead of being
redrawn — which is why the mean frame delta is 0.24 for something with this much
structure on screen. At every second sample the tangent is crossed with the vertical axis
to get a ribbon normal, and the two edges plus a rung are built in 3D and then projected,
so the sheet twists and goes edge-on exactly where the trajectory rolls from one lobe to
the other. The twist is the geometry of the flow, not an effect laid over it, and the
rungs give the ribbon a woven surface that reads at a glance. Hue follows height on the
attractor and brightness follows age as `0.10 + 0.72 age^2`, so the leading edge glows and
the tail sinks into black; ribbon width breathes 18% on a slow sine and the view yaws
steadily while nodding on a 3.6-minute cycle. 248 fps, mean frame delta 0.24, 75%
near-black — overlay.
