# 189 — Reiter Crystal

A snowflake grown by the real rule, not drawn. Reiter's 2005 hexagonal automaton
runs on a 209×189 triangular lattice: a cell is RECEPTIVE if it or any of its six
neighbours already holds one unit of ice; receptive cells lock their water away
and gain a constant vapour influx γ, while everything else diffuses with
u += (α/2)·(mean₆(u) − u). That single local rule, started from one seed cell in a
uniform vapour field β, produces genuine snow-crystal morphology — and genuine
six-fold symmetry, because the lattice and the seed have it and the rule is
local. Nudging β and γ per segment walks the crystal across Nakaya's diagram: fat
hexagonal plates, sectored stars, or fully branched stellar dendrites with
side-branch trains. Freezing time is stored per cell and drives hue, so the
finished flake carries growth rings like a tree, and a twelve-frame display lag
makes every cell's freeze a fade-in rather than a flip (measured motion 0.02).
One step every other frame grows the crystal to the lattice edge over a segment,
after which it holds and turns slowly. Accumulator: clears at sl == 0. 239 fps
(212 fps averaged across the growth phase), delta 0.02.
