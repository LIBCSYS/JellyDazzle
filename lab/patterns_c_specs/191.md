# 191 — Hopf Weave

The Hopf fibration, drawn as the linked circles it actually is. Every point b on the
2-sphere owns a great circle in the 3-sphere; lift it with
`q(u) = ((1+z)cos u, x sin u - y cos u, x cos u + y sin u, (1+z)sin u) / sqrt(2(1+z))`
and stereographically project by `(q1,q2,q3)/(1-q4)`, and that circle lands in R3 as a
Villarceau circle lying on a torus. Any two fibres are linked exactly once, so a small
circle of thirty base points on S2 lifts to thirty rings that thread each other into a
single woven torus. The base latitude breathes between about 0.7 and 1.6 radians, which
swells and thins the torus; its axis tilts on a slow sine, and the whole solid turns in
perspective. Samples whose `1-q4` falls under 0.13 are dropped and the polyline is
broken there, which is what keeps a fibre passing near the projection pole from firing
a line across the frame. A brightness wave with two lobes runs around each ring at a
different phase per fibre, so the weave keeps moving while the geometry barely changes.
Line art on black, 132 samples a fibre into a 480x360 float canvas, one 5-tap glow, then
bilinear to full resolution. 212 fps, mean frame delta 1.57, 83% near-black — overlay.
