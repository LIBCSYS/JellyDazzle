# 183 — String Hyperboloid

The string-sculpture proof that a doubly-curved surface can be made of nothing but
straight lines. Two rings of 44 pins sit at z = ±H; pin i on the top ring is joined
by a taut straight string to pin i+τ on the bottom, and a second family runs to
pin i−τ. Every string is dead straight, yet the envelope they sweep is the
hyperboloid of one sheet x²+y²−(z/k)² = R², whose waist radius is R·cos(τ/2). As τ
breathes on a slow sine the solid inflates toward a cylinder, pinches through the
double cone at τ = π, and opens again, while the two families cross into a woven
diamond lattice that thickens and thins with the waist. It is drawn in real 3-D:
each string is sampled 330 times, rotated by a spin and a nodding tilt, divided by
a true perspective term, and splatted bilinearly, with depth setting brightness so
the far wall of the solid sinks into the black. A travelling sine runs light up
each filament like current in a wire, and the two pin rings are stroked faintly to
close the sculpture. Line art on black, ~92% near-zero. 226 fps, delta 0.75.
