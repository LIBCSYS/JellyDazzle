# 152 Pentagrid Lattice

**Look.** A wireframe Penrose tiling — fat and thin rhombs locking into five-fold
rosettes and long "worms" that never repeat — turning very slowly over black, while a
broad plane wave of light drifts across it so whole districts of the lattice brighten
and dim in turn. Thin rhombs carry the accent hue, fat rhombs the base hue; cell
interiors stay at zero, so it is pure line art and stacks cleanly under MAX.

**Math.** De Bruijn's dual (pentagrid) construction. Five line families with unit normals
`e_j = (cos 2pi j/5, sin 2pi j/5)` and offsets `gamma_j` summing to zero; for every pair
`j<k` and every pair of integers `(p,q)` the intersection `z` of line `p` of family `j`
with line `q` of family `k` is solved from the 2x2 system, the remaining indices are read
off as `K_m = ceil(e_m . z + gamma_m)`, and the rhomb's corners are
`V = SUM_m K_m e_m`, `V+e_j`, `V+e_j+e_k`, `V+e_k` — one rhomb per intersection,
aperiodic by construction. That geometry (~2-6k rhombs inside a radius cutoff) is built
once per segment and cached; each frame only rotates and rescales it (0.0016 rad/frame,
+-9% breathing) and evaluates a travelling brightness wave `0.34 + 0.66 sin(d . c - wt)`
per rhomb centre. Edges are drawn by additive bilinear line-splatting into a 480x360
float canvas, softened with one 5-tap blur, tone-mapped `1-exp(-x)` and upscaled.
Measured 197-312 fps at 1280x960, delta 1.88-3.22.
