# 160 Chrome Relief

**Look.** Hammered, oil-slicked metal: a hexagonal relief of ridges and dimples with a
ring wave rolling out of the centre, lit by three coloured lights that crawl slowly
around it, throwing hard specular glints along every crest and pooling deep colour in the
valleys. Full-bleed, glossy and saturated — this is a GROUND layer, the thing the sparse
wire routines (151-157) are meant to sit on top of.

**Math.** The classic DOS bump-map, done with an analytic normal instead of a difference
image. The height field is six travelling plane waves on three hexagonal axes (three long
62-132 px, three short 30-62 px) plus one radial ring wave; because h is a sum of sines,
`grad h` is a sum of cosines with the same phases, so the normal
`N = normalize(-dh/dx, -dh/dy, 1)` costs seven table lookups and one reciprocal square
root per pixel and is exactly noise-free. Lighting is three coloured *directional* lights
taken from three palette stops (directional, so no per-pixel light vector: their
directions rotate on incommensurate orbits at 0.0013-0.0023 rad/frame with a breathing
elevation), each contributing Lambert `max(0, N.L)^2` and a Blinn specular
`max(0, N.H)^32` computed by five squarings. On top of that a chrome environment term
samples the palette by the normal's x-tilt and weights it by `1 - N.z`, which is what
gives the rim of every ridge its iridescent edge. The relief amplitude breathes +-15%.
440x330 canvas, upscaled. Measured 146-218 fps at 1280x960, delta 0.27-0.70.
