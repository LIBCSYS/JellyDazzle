# 196 — Craquelure

A crack network writing itself into a black glaze. Twenty-six crack tips creep forward
0.62 pixels a frame, wandering by up to 0.02 radians a step so cracks run nearly straight,
and each stamps its own trail into an occupancy grid eight steps behind itself — the lag
is what stops a tip tripping over its own tail. A tip probes 3.2 pixels ahead and dies the
moment it meets anybody's trail, which is the one rule that turns random walks into real
craquelure: cracks terminate on cracks and never cross, so the frame fills with T
junctions and closed cells instead of a scribble. Tips branch at right angles with 0.9%
probability a frame, inheriting hue and 82% of the parent's weight, and replacement tips
are seeded preferentially on an existing crack — which is how mud cells actually close.
The canvas is a true accumulator: it clears when `sl == 0` or when segment-local time jumps,
then builds for the rest of the segment, losing 0.07% of its light a frame so old lattices
sink away under new ones and nothing ever saturates. Nothing else in the plug-in set moves
this little: mean frame delta 0.01, which is 800 times under the house budget, and the
picture still visibly grows over a segment — 73% near-black at frame 400, 69% by frame
1800. 276 fps. Overlay, and the natural partner for anything with a bright field.
