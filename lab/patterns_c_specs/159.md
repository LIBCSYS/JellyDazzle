# 159 Caustic Veil

**Look.** The net of light on the floor of a shallow pool: bright filaments that braid,
pinch into cusps and re-open, over dark green-blue water, with faint prismatic fringes
along the sharpest folds. Mostly dark with a sparse bright web — a natural overlay, and
the only routine in the set that is a genuine light-transport simulation.

**Math.** The surface is `h(x,y,t) = SUM_i A_i sin(k_i . x - w_i t)` over four travelling
waves (wavelengths 34-112 canvas px, dispersive speeds `w ~ sqrt(k)`, deliberately slowed
so the pattern evolves over tens of seconds). At small angles Snell's law deflects a
vertical ray in proportion to the surface gradient, so each point of a photon grid — one
photon per canvas pixel, jittered by a static blue-ish noise table to kill grid moire —
is displaced by `-d grad h` and deposited with bilinear splatting on the floor buffer.
Where the surface is locally convex the photons pile up and the caustic filaments appear
by themselves: folds and cusps of the projection map, not a drawn curve. The three
channels use slightly different deflection constants (+-5-14%), which is dispersion, and
is why the fold edges break into colour exactly as real water does. Phases advance
incrementally along each row, so the cost per photon is four sine-table lookups. One
5-tap blur, exponential tone map, upscale from 440x330. Measured 196-242 fps at
1280x960, delta 0.81-1.86.
